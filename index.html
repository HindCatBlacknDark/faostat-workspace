Understand what you are measuring

Availability ≠ consumption

These are national nutrient availability measures based on supply utilization accounts. They do not measure what individual people eat, and national averages do not show inequalities within a country.

Know the limits

Cereals alone cannot establish overall diet quality or their share of total energy. Changes over time do not establish causes. Every exported observation has flag E, whose supplied description is “Estimated value.”

How availability is estimated

FAO combines national food supply and utilization statistics to estimate food available for people. Nutrient conversion tables connect food quantities to nutrient amounts. Per-person estimates use the population for the corresponding year. These national estimates do not describe how food is distributed among people or the amounts they actually eat. The Global Nutrient Conversion Table (NCT) draws on 13 food composition tables selected from around the world. Their quality was assessed using the FAO/INFOODS Evaluation Framework for food composition tables and databases. This quality screening concerns the composition data; it does not make national availability a measurement of individual intake.

How national data are collected

National data are collected through questionnaires, official websites, official trade data, publications, specialized institutions and literature review. This domain-level description was supplied by the user; it does not identify which source was used for each country/year observation.

What belongs to the cereals group?

The published food grouping includes rice, maize, wheat, sorghum, millet and other cereals, with derived products. Its boundaries exclude some cereal-based snacks. The classification is a reference for the group; the aggregate CSV cannot reveal the commodities or proportions behind a country’s value.

What does flag E mean?

E — Estimated value is supplied directly in every CSV row. It is not a confidence interval, and the CSV does not identify the estimation procedure for each observation.

Full domain versus this exercise

The supplied domain metadata describes 178 countries and territories, using FAOSTAT geography and UN M49 codes, with nutrients disaggregated by FAO/WHO GIFT food groups. The active cereals exercise contains only the 43 countries present in the original CSV, for 2019–2023. Domain coverage does not imply that every country has data for every year.

What the supplemental files tell us

The supplemental result contains Calcium supply (4009) for All food groups (FG0), in mg/cap/d: 2,411 observations across 178 countries and territories, 2010–2023. There are 81 absent country–year combinations within that grid, no duplicate observation keys and no blank values. Every row is labelled Estimated value. These observations are kept separate from the cereals energy and fibre exercise. The SQL file is a SELECT query template with food-group and indicator parameters; their bindings are not recorded. It does not document nutrient calculations.
